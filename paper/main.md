# Source-Supported HSE–Laplace Posterior Inference for Cross-Dataset Industrial Diagnosis

## Abstract

Industrial vibration datasets observe related mechanical processes through different sampling, bandwidth and sensing arrangements. These differences change which latent components are constrained by a measurement, making unrestricted alignment or completion an ambiguous basis for cross-dataset diagnosis. We formulate the problem as inference over a source-supported latent space, separating common-observable, observed-private, source-supported-missing and source-global-null components. A source-supported component is admitted to conditional generation only when the source observation design identifies the required joint law. The proposed HSE–LLapDiff model uses acquisition-aware physical and conditional-moment features to condition a projected latent diffusion process, with stable Laplace-domain dynamics on the eligible missing component. Observed evidence is retained, and globally unsupported coordinates are excluded from recovery outputs. The analysis distinguishes likelihood support from prior-mediated inference, relates condition compression to posterior and denoising error, and specifies a support-preserving generation constraint. Controlled comparisons separate the roles of the conditioner, the Laplace parameterization, the generative family and the diagnostic consumer. The primary evaluation target is diagnosis on unseen industrial datasets; posterior accuracy and uncertainty are separate mechanism outcomes rather than substitutes for diagnostic evidence.

## 1. Introduction

Industrial fault diagnosis increasingly draws on datasets collected on different machines, at different sampling rates and through different sensing chains. These datasets can share fault categories while exposing different parts of a mechanical response. A representation learned from a high-bandwidth recording may use an informative component that is absent from a lower-bandwidth deployment measurement. HSE already provides an interface for heterogeneous fault signals, and FISHER develops multimodal industrial representations using rate-dependent time–frequency structure [@li2025hse; @fan2026fisher]. The remaining issue is not input compatibility alone: a transferable diagnosis system must distinguish evidence that is measured, evidence that can be inferred under an identified source model, and components for which the source data provide no direct likelihood information.

Existing representation methods establish several relevant capabilities. Temporal/contextual contrasting and time–frequency consistency learn transferable features through structured augmentations and cross-view objectives [@eldele2021tstcc; @zhang2022tfc]. Shared/private representation learning preserves domain-dependent features alongside a common representation [@bousmalis2016dsn]. These capabilities motivate retaining, rather than indiscriminately aligning away, currently observed private evidence. However, statistical invariance is not a substitute for a common label mechanism: invariant-representation analysis and domain-generalization experiments show that conditional mismatch and model selection materially affect transfer [@zhao2019invariant; @gulrajani2021domainbed]. A source-only cross-dataset protocol must therefore separate acquisition changes from changes in machine dynamics, fault ontology and operating condition.

Partial-observation generative models provide a complementary capability. Multimodal variational models infer missing views from available modalities, while conditional diffusion and state-space diffusion learn distributions over missing time-series values [@wu2018mvae; @shi2019mmvae; @tashiro2021csdi; @alcaraz2023sssd]. In inverse problems, DDRM and DDNM distinguish measured and unmeasured directions, and DPS combines a generative prior with a noisy measurement likelihood [@kawar2022ddrm; @wang2023ddnm; @chung2023dps]. Thus, generating missing components or projecting an update onto a measurement-null space is already established. The industrial difficulty is that the prior itself is learned from incomplete, heterogeneous source data: the current acquisition's null space need not coincide with the part of the latent space that the source experiment can identify.

Learning from corrupted observations makes this distinction precise. Ambient Diffusion and its consistent extension establish recovery under stated corruption and identifiability conditions; A-DPS subsequently uses a prior learned from corrupted data for inverse problems with a different measurement operator [@daras2023ambient; @daras2024consistent; @aali2025ambientdps]. These results preclude a blanket assertion that paired clean views are always necessary. They also do not license recovery from an arbitrary collection of unpaired industrial marginals. The relevant requirement is identification of the conditional law used at deployment, through paired source events, an identifiable observation ensemble, or an explicit physical coupling. Moreover, a learned latent coordinate is not automatically a mechanical mode: latent-variable identifiability results and disentanglement counterexamples require additional structure before attaching physical support labels to coordinates [@khemakhem2020ivae; @locatello2019disentanglement].

This motivates the distinction in Fig. 1. A component may be missing in the current acquisition but supported by the source experiment, or it may be invisible to every source operator. The first can become a posterior target when its conditional law is identified; the second cannot acquire a data-supported recovery claim merely because a generator produces a value. An unobserved coordinate can nevertheless change under a correlated prior, so rejection is a declaration of the inference scope, not a theorem that its marginal posterior must equal its prior. Our question is whether a source-supported conditional posterior, learned without target-dataset fitting, can improve diagnosis on a new industrial dataset while preserving this distinction.

Three scientific challenges follow. **First, support must be meaningful across datasets.** Rate metadata or nonzero coordinate sensitivity does not establish recoverability, and unknown sensor/dynamics changes can invalidate a common latent basis. The method therefore needs a source-established coordinate correspondence and explicit observability operators. **Second, inference must preserve its evidential scope.** Conditional identifiability, posterior approximation and numerical support preservation are different requirements; a generative update must not erase their distinction. **Third, posterior usefulness must be separated from conditioner and consumer effects.** Better probabilistic scores can coexist with unchanged or worse classification, and a statistical prefix can help simply through ordinary nonlinear reparameterization. Each challenge requires its own measurement rather than a single pooled accuracy.

We propose an acquisition-support-aware HSE–LLapDiff formulation. HSE supplies a structured condition containing observed features, acquisition descriptors, support information and a conditional-moment anchor. Latent Laplace Diffusion supplies the conditional generative mechanism on the eligible missing subspace. Its Laplace component is a stable continuous-time modal parameterization, inherited from LLapDiff and related to Neural Laplace; it is not Laplace-distributed perturbation noise [@holt2022neurallaplace; @you2026llapdiff]. The ordinary Gaussian diffusion mechanism remains a distinct ingredient [@ho2020ddpm]. This pairing is motivated by event-local mechanical dynamics and arbitrary-time evaluation, but its independent value must be tested against an ordinary latent denoiser, deterministic completion and Gaussian/mixture posteriors. A posterior-sample diagnostic head integrates uncertainty; a moment-only summary remains a separate, potentially lossy choice.

The proposed study has three contributions. **(1)** A source-referenced formulation specifies four observational roles and the additional identifiability condition that admits a missing component to the inference target. **(2)** An HSE-conditioned, support-restricted LLapDiff mechanism couples structured observations with stable latent dynamics while preserving the observed-evidence path and excluding unsupported recovery outputs. **(3)** A theory-linked attribution design connects condition compression, posterior approximation and support compliance to separately measured cross-dataset diagnostic effects. Same-head affine and generic nonlinear controls isolate the statistical anchor; matched generative families isolate the posterior mechanism. These contributions concern a specified formulation and method design. Industrial performance claims require the corresponding unseen-dataset evidence, not the existence of the component models or their analytical special cases.
