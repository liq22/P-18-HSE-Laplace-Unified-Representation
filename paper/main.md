# Statistically Anchored HSE Conditioning for Industrial Fault Diagnosis

## Abstract

Industrial vibration measurements of related mechanical events differ in sampling rate, observation duration and missingness. We study whether a conditional-moment reparameterization of frozen HSE features improves fault diagnosis beyond an equally supervised ordinary code. A shared trunk and global moment head are selected using source data and then frozen. The candidate message replaces a fixed prefix with estimated mean and covariance-factor coordinates while preserving the ordinary tail. We analyze the actual transformation rather than assuming that a deterministic message is necessarily lossy: under an explicit rank condition the complete message is invertible. A same-head affine control separates access to the trained head from the nonlinear covariance parameterization without adding supervision or parameters. The resulting evaluation uses direct diagnostic consumers, original-recording separation, generic coordinate controls and measured computation. Native Laplace-diffusion loss and probabilistic scores serve as secondary mechanism diagnostics, not as guarantees for the primary fault-classification endpoint.

## 1. Introduction

Industrial fault diagnosis operates on sensor measurements rather than directly observed fault mechanisms. Sampling rate, sensor response, channel availability and observation duration affect the evidence available to a model. Physics-informed learning motivates retaining the measurement structure [@karniadakis2021physics], while representation learning relates useful coordinates to a prediction task [@bengio2013representation]. HSE already supplies a heterogeneous fault-signal interface, and FISHER and TF-ProFM are direct industrial representation references [@li2025hse; @fan2026fisher; @pi2026tfprofm]. Our question is not first industrial unification, but whether an explicit statistical coordinate system helps a finite diagnosis model when its information access and supervision are held fixed.

Acquisition changes must be distinguished from changes of physical operating condition. Resampling and observation loss change accessible evidence, whereas speed, load, sensor placement and machine variation can also alter the underlying response. Time-aware models demonstrate ways to retain temporal structure [@kidger2020cde; @zhang2024tpatchgnn], but this does not determine which conditioner is preferable for fault diagnosis. Original-recording separation, source-only preprocessing and the same deployment side information are required to isolate the representation effect.

Statistical compression and goal-oriented posterior approximation already address retained evidence and target quantities [@alsing2018compression; @spantini2015lowrank; @spantini2017goal]. Approximate sufficiency and predictive V-information likewise provide established perspectives on conditional generation and computationally restricted consumers [@oko2025sufficiency; @xu2020usable]. We therefore do not interpret a derived message M=T(R) as containing additional observations. Its usefulness depends on the implemented map, the finite consumer, its regularization and training. The affine mean readout cannot enlarge an affine diagnosis family, and the complete statistical-prefix message need not discard information if it admits an inverse.

Our intervention uses one shared source-trained path. The Gaussian conditional score updates the trunk whose ordinary code R is consumed by the strongest comparator. After checkpoint selection, the entire input, trunk and head path is frozen. In addition to R and M, we transmit the raw output of that same affine head with the same tail. This control uses no extra parameters or supervision and omits only the nonlinear covariance-coordinate transformation. It makes the question more specific than comparing a supervised message against an unmodified or weakly trained code. Full-dimensional PCA, orthogonal transforms, whitening and a small learned reparameterization remain alternative explanations to test.

Fault diagnosis is the primary task. Direct linear, small-MLP and matched neural diagnostic heads precede any necessity claim for an iterative generative model. Neural Laplace, CSDI and LLapDiff provide inherited continuous-time and conditional-generation mechanisms [@holt2022neurallaplace; @tashiro2021csdi; @you2026llapdiff]. Here LLapDiff is a secondary finite consumer of the same condition. Its squared denoising loss and latent Energy Score do not guarantee improved macro-F1. The global head estimates conditional moments through a declared scoring objective; positive definiteness alone does not identify a full posterior or ensure finite optimization and unseen-acquisition calibration [@gneiting2007proper; @seitzer2022pitfalls].

All empirical method evaluation for this TII manuscript uses industrial datasets prepared and split by PHMFactory. The primary endpoint is recording-balanced pooled-confusion macro-F1, with conventional pooled-window metrics and actual computational cost reported separately. Best source-selected single representation and static predictive fusion are required before optional acquisition routing. Reference data-path acceptance is documented under experimental setup, not counted as a learned method result. General multi-domain experiments and their theoretical interpretation are maintained in the companion TPAMI workspace with explicit result ownership.

## 2. Contribution under evaluation

The candidate contribution is the concrete conditional-moment coordinate intervention under matched industrial supervision, inputs and consumers. Its supporting analysis characterizes the actual map and defines controls that can refute a statistical-specific interpretation. Genuine industrial comparisons must show more than a benefit from auxiliary supervision, ordinary affine coordinates, generic nonlinear features or extra computation. Existing analytical tests and framework acceptance do not establish that method advantage.
