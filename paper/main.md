# Source-Supported HSE–Laplace Posterior Inference for Cross-Dataset Industrial Diagnosis

## Abstract

Industrial vibration datasets expose different parts of a mechanical response because sensing, bandwidth and sampling arrangements vary. Completing a missing component therefore requires more than a compatible input representation: the source experiment must identify its distribution under the condition actually available at deployment. We formulate cross-dataset diagnosis through source-referenced common-observable, observed-private, source-supported-missing and source-global-null components. A finite counterexample shows that even overlapping source views can identify a missing component conditional on their common observation without identifying it conditional on all currently observed evidence. We propose an HSE–LLapDiff construction that qualifies the inference target together with its condition, preserves the observed-evidence path, and restricts a Gaussian latent diffusion process to the admitted missing subspace. Stable Laplace-domain dynamics parameterize temporal predictions within that process. A crossed restriction–dynamics experiment separates their individual effects and interaction, while same-head conditioner controls isolate the statistical anchor. The evaluation distinguishes posterior quality, admitted coverage, diagnostic performance and computation. The central hypothesis is that restricting the posterior to source-identified targets can make temporal generation useful for diagnosis on unseen industrial datasets, rather than merely producing plausible completions.

## 1. Introduction

Industrial diagnosis draws on measurements collected across machines and sensing arrangements. A fault category may be shared across datasets while its recorded evidence changes with sensor response, bandwidth, observation duration and sampling. HSE provides a heterogeneous fault-signal interface, and FISHER develops industrial representations from sampling-rate-dependent time–frequency structure [@li2025hse; @fan2026fisher]. These capabilities make heterogeneous inputs usable. They also expose a more specific inference problem: when a deployment recording lacks a component used by the source models, which part of that component can be inferred from the remaining evidence?

Representation learning supplies useful but different answers to this problem. Temporal/contextual and time–frequency contrasting learn transferable features from structured views [@eldele2021tstcc; @zhang2022tfc]. Shared/private representations retain domain-specific information alongside common features [@bousmalis2016dsn]. Yet marginal alignment does not establish a shared prediction mechanism, and the choice of validation domains changes what a transfer result measures [@zhao2019invariant; @gulrajani2021domainbed]. For industrial diagnosis, acquisition loss must therefore be separated from changes in mechanical response, fault ontology and operating condition. Currently observed private evidence may remain useful even when it cannot be aligned across every source.

Probabilistic models offer an alternative to forcing all observations into a common feature set. Multimodal variational models infer missing views, and conditional diffusion models generate missing time-series values [@wu2018mvae; @shi2019mmvae; @tashiro2021csdi; @alcaraz2023sssd]. DDRM and DDNM use measurement structure to distinguish constrained and unconstrained directions; DPS combines a diffusion prior with approximate noisy-likelihood guidance [@kawar2022ddrm; @wang2023ddnm; @chung2023dps]. These methods establish that missingness and measurement operators can organize generation. The remaining issue here concerns the evidence supporting the learned conditional distribution, not the availability of a masking or projection operation.

Learning from corrupted sources already addresses part of that issue. Ambient Diffusion derives a corrupted-data denoising objective under conditions on the observation ensemble, and its consistent extension studies distribution recovery from noisy-only data [@daras2023ambient; @daras2024consistent]. A-DPS uses an ambient prior for inverse reconstruction. Diffusion EM learns a prior through posterior-sampling and refitting steps, while the DiffEM preprint directly trains a conditional diffusion model within an EM procedure [@aali2025ambientdps; @rozet2024diem; @hosseintabar2025diffem]. Thus, neither corrupted-source learning nor posterior inference from incomplete training observations defines the gap. What must be specified is the particular joint law identified by the source observation design and required by the downstream diagnosis.

Figure 1 in Section 2 isolates this ambiguity. Suppose one source observes a common component and a private component, whereas another observes the common component and a component missing from the current acquisition. Two joint models can agree on both observed source distributions but imply opposite missing-component predictions after conditioning on the private component. A shared observation is not automatically a statistical separator. Consequently, a conditional learned for the common component alone cannot simply be relabelled as the conditional given the complete HSE input. Moreover, assigning these roles to latent coordinates requires a common reference: latent identifiability results do not support interpreting arbitrary VAE coordinates as mechanical modes [@khemakhem2020ivae; @locatello2019disentanglement].


This leads to three scientific challenges. **First, establish a comparable inference target across acquisitions.** Coordinate correspondence and the required joint conditional must be justified together; observing each component somewhere is insufficient. **Second, preserve that target through temporal generation.** Support restriction and a temporal inductive bias act on different aspects of the posterior, and their interaction must be distinguished from the effects of either alone. **Third, determine whether posterior modeling helps diagnosis.** An accurate conditional distribution need not improve a finite diagnostic model, while extra supervision, nonlinear coordinates or computation can explain an apparent gain. Each challenge requires a corresponding controlled contrast.

We propose an acquisition-aware HSE–LLapDiff construction for this setting (Fig. 2). HSE retains observed features, timestamps and acquisition descriptors, with a source-trained conditional-moment anchor. The inference target is selected together with the complete condition under a declared source observation model. A fixed projector restricts latent diffusion to that target; a separate observed-evidence path carries measured information and its uncertainty. LLapDiff supplies a stable continuous-time modal parameterization, motivated by event-local damped dynamics and irregular query times [@holt2022neurallaplace; @you2026llapdiff]. Its Gaussian diffusion process and Laplace-domain temporal structure are distinct ingredients [@ho2020ddpm]. Their value is tested against ordinary latent diffusion and simpler conditional distributions under the same observation access.

The contributions are threefold. **(1) Conditioning-qualified formulation:** we define a source-referenced partial-posterior target together with the conditioning information that identifies it, and exhibit the failure of extending a common-view conditional to the full observed input without additional coupling information. **(2) Support-restricted temporal inference:** we specify an HSE-conditioned LLapDiff process that confines generative updates to an admitted latent subspace while retaining observed evidence and uncertainty. **(3) Mechanism-resolved evaluation:** we derive a restriction–dynamics factorial comparison and link it to fixed-head conditioner controls, same-target posterior scores and source-only cross-dataset diagnosis. The formulation and support properties are analytical contributions; diagnostic benefit is the hypothesis tested by the industrial experiments.
