# Statistically Anchored Conditioning for Latent Laplace Diffusion

## Abstract

A shared tensor interface does not ensure that heterogeneous time-series evidence is represented in a form that a conditional generator can readily use. We study a statistically anchored conditioning intervention for HSE-based Latent Laplace Diffusion. A shared ordinary code is trained with a global Gaussian conditional score to predict moments of a declared reference-latent functional. After source-validation selection, the code and statistical readout are frozen. The candidate replaces a fixed subset of code coordinates with explicit moment fields and retains the remaining ordinary features. Its primary comparator transmits the entire ordinary code trained through the same supervision. This deliberately nested comparison excludes an increase in Bayes information and isolates a possible reduction in finite-denoiser fitting cost. The analysis separates that fitting effect from information loss, identifies the effect of an explicit covariance floor, and retains a smooth same-moment counterexample to moment-only sufficiency. Native component checks verify gradient connectivity, field consumption and loss conventions. A small paired experiment on genuine frozen HSE and reference-encoder outputs is the decisive empirical test; the current analytical and component evidence does not establish a learned-method advantage.

## 1. Introduction

Industrial monitoring observes physical processes through sensors, filters and sampling schedules. A representation must accommodate differences in the measured signal and in the evidence available about the process. Physics-informed machine learning motivates the use of scientific structure in data-driven models [@karniadakis2021physics], while representation learning asks which factors remain accessible to downstream computation [@bengio2013representation]. HSE provides a common embedding interface for heterogeneous fault-diagnosis signals [@li2025hse]. Here we ask a narrower question: under a fixed conditioning budget, can an explicitly supervised statistical readout make target-relevant information easier for a finite conditional generator to use?

Time-series methods already address important aspects of heterogeneous observations. PatchTST groups sequences into local patches [@nie2023patchtst], Neural CDE models irregular observations through continuous-time dynamics [@kidger2020cde], and t-PatchGNN combines transformable patches with cross-variable modeling [@zhang2024tpatchgnn]. ContiFormer integrates continuous-time attention [@chen2023contiformer]. More recent Hi-Patch uses hierarchical patch graphs across variable sampling scales [@luo2025hipatch], while HyperIMTS links observations through temporal and variable hyperedges [@li2025hyperimts]. Time-IMM distinguishes causes of irregular and asynchronous measurement [@chang2025timeimm]. These advances do not remove the need to state what a subsequent probabilistic model actually receives: timestamps, masks, side information and observed duration are part of the condition, not incidental metadata.

Probabilistic modeling provides a complementary capability. Neural Laplace learns dynamics in the Laplace domain [@holt2022neurallaplace], and CSDI conditions diffusion on observed values for probabilistic imputation [@tashiro2021csdi]. Latent diffusion reduces the generative dimension through a learned autoencoder [@rombach2022latent]. LLapDiff combines latent-trajectory diffusion, stable modal prediction and gap-aware history conditioning for irregular multivariate time series [@you2026llapdiff]. We retain its generative mechanism. Its reference latent, its predicted modal parameters and the physical coefficients of a known-pole oracle are different objects. Improved approximation of the last object does not by itself establish better prediction of the first.

Statistical compression offers strong precedents. Score compression can preserve Fisher information under stated assumptions [@alsing2018compression]; approximate-sufficiency theory links compressed conditions to conditional generation [@oko2025sufficiency]. Prior-aware and goal-oriented Bayesian approximation account explicitly for prior information and the quantity of interest [@spantini2015lowrank; @spantini2017goal]. Thus neither retaining more matrix entries nor projecting posterior moments to a target is a new general principle. Moreover, side information may already reconstruct the acquisition operator. In that case an explicit field can improve finite computation without adding observations or recovering previously missing Bayes information.

Statistical interpretation introduces a separate difficulty. A free denoiser can compensate an invertible change of its input coordinates, so a field named mean or covariance does not acquire that meaning from denoising loss alone. Supervised intermediate concepts illustrate the role of an explicit semantic objective [@koh2020concept], although concept supervision is not a posterior theorem. Gaussian conditional scoring identifies first and second moments at an unrestricted population optimum [@gneiting2007proper], but exact moments can still conceal non-Gaussian shape. Finite heteroscedastic likelihood optimization can also yield poor estimates [@seitzer2022pitfalls]. The training objective, interpretation of a frozen readout, and information delivered to the generator must therefore be examined separately.

We consider a single conditioning intervention with a strong control. A trainable trunk forms an ordinary code; a global moment head reads this same code, so the auxiliary gradient reaches the path later consumed by the ordinary baseline. Both arms use one source-selected checkpoint and freeze the complete conditioning path. The candidate sends explicit moments plus the remaining ordinary coordinates; the comparator sends the complete supervised ordinary code. The candidate message is consequently a deterministic function of the comparator message. Our analysis decomposes the difference in actual prediction risk into a nonnegative Bayes-information penalty and a finite-fitting difference. A candidate advantage, if observed, must overcome the former rather than being attributed to additional Bayes information.

The method contribution under investigation is this statistically anchored code replacement, not the generic probability identities used to analyze it. The empirical comparison fixes the generation target, supervision, message size and native diffusion objective. Prediction parameterization, time sampling and loss weighting matter because they determine which denoising errors are optimized [@nichol2021improved; @song2021maximum]. We distinguish source holdout from unseen acquisition, retain moment-only and equally supervised ordinary controls, and measure the computation of both conditioning and generation. The resulting experiment can support either a useful finite-computation intervention or a simpler ordinary-condition solution; a reduction in moment-readout loss alone is insufficient.
