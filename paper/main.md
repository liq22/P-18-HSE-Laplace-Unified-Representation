# Statistically Anchored HSE Conditioning for Industrial Fault Diagnosis

## Abstract

Industrial vibration records of related mechanical events can differ in sampling rate, observation duration and missingness. A common embedding interface does not ensure that a finite diagnostic or generative model can use the retained evidence equally well. We study a statistically anchored HSE condition for Latent Laplace Diffusion and downstream fault diagnosis. An ordinary code and a global conditional-moment readout share source supervision; after checkpoint selection, the entire conditioning path is frozen. A fixed-size message replaces part of the ordinary code with the statistical readout while retaining the remaining features. Its deterministic relationship to the equally supervised ordinary code separates possible information loss from finite-model fitting effects. Industrial evaluation is defined through PHMFactory with original-recording separation, explicit acquisition changes and independently recomputed diagnosis metrics. The study tests whether this concrete statistical reparameterization improves the same downstream model without attributing gains to extra observations, supervision or unreported computation.

## 1. Introduction

Industrial fault diagnosis observes mechanical processes through acquisition systems rather than accessing their physical state directly. Sampling rate, sensor response, channel availability and observation duration affect the vibration evidence provided to a model. Physics-informed learning motivates making the measurement process explicit [@karniadakis2021physics], while representation learning relates useful coordinates to the prediction task [@bengio2013representation]. HSE already provides a heterogeneous-signal interface for fault-diagnosis models [@li2025hse], and industrial foundation-model studies such as FISHER and TF-ProFM provide direct application references [@fan2026fisher; @pi2026tfprofm]. Our question is not first industrial unification, but how a finite downstream model uses a fixed-budget representation under declared industrial acquisition changes.

Acquisition heterogeneity differs from a change of operating condition. Resampling or losing observations changes the available evidence; speed, load, sensor placement and machine variation can change the physical response itself. These factors must not be combined into a single random-window accuracy table. Continuous-time and irregular-series methods illustrate ways of retaining temporal structure [@kidger2020cde; @zhang2024tpatchgnn], but their existence does not establish which conditioner is preferable for the same fault-diagnosis or latent-generation task. The industrial comparison must hold actual side inputs, target definition and original-recording separation fixed.

We retain Latent Laplace Diffusion as the generative component, rather than introducing a second diffusion architecture. Neural Laplace and CSDI provide earlier continuous-time and conditional-generation foundations [@holt2022neurallaplace; @tashiro2021csdi]. LLapDiff predicts a reference-encoder latent trajectory using stable modal dynamics and an iterative reverse process [@you2026llapdiff]. A known-pole coefficient oracle, that reference latent, and denoiser-predicted modal parameters are distinct objects. Better analytical coefficient reconstruction alone cannot establish better diagnosis or calibrated prediction on industrial recordings.

Statistical supervision offers an interpretable readout, but neither its name nor covariance positive definiteness guarantees its meaning. Score compression and goal-oriented posterior approximation already study information and target-aware summaries [@alsing2018compression; @spantini2015lowrank; @spantini2017goal]. Approximate sufficiency and computationally restricted usable information are also established topics [@oko2025sufficiency; @xu2020usable]. We therefore compare a complete statistically supervised ordinary code R against a deterministic same-budget message M=T(R). A gain cannot be attributed to new Bayes information: the explicit readout must improve finite-model fitting enough to compensate for any information discarded by the message. Gaussian scoring supplies a population moment objective, while finite heteroscedastic optimization and out-of-domain residuals are checked directly [@gneiting2007proper; @seitzer2022pitfalls].

The proposed industrial intervention is minimal. A global moment head reads the same trainable trunk that is later consumed by the ordinary-code comparator, so auxiliary supervision does not train only a detached branch. After source-only checkpoint selection, HSE, preprocessing, trunk and moment head are frozen. Both arms receive the same observation masks, reference targets and native LLapDiff objective. The study first compares M with this strongest single-code control; best single selection and static predictive fusion remain practical industrial references. Acquisition-conditioned selection is an optional measured ablation, not a required module or a claimed new routing theorem.

All empirical datasets for this TII manuscript are industrial and pass through PHMFactory's accepted preparation, labels and splits. The primary target is recording-balanced fault-diagnosis performance; latent probabilistic scores are reported separately as mechanism diagnostics. The exact MFPT reference has been executed and independently checked, but it is not a result of the proposed learned conditioner. Learned HSE comparisons, broader industrial acquisitions and industrial strong baselines will determine whether the intervention is retained. General multi-domain representation and policy-selection studies are developed separately and are not counted as TII empirical evidence.

## 2. Contributions under evaluation

The candidate contribution is a shared-supervision, fixed-budget statistical HSE message tested for industrial fault diagnosis using the same finite consumer. The analytical contribution is a method-specific interpretation of its information–fitting trade-off under explicit assumptions, not a new general information identity. An empirical contribution requires genuine industrial HSE/reference exports, strong matched controls and recording-level evidence; reference-framework acceptance and synthetic numerical witnesses do not establish that contribution.
