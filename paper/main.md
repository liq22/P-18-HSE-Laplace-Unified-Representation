# Target-Aware Posterior Conditioning for Heterogeneous Time Series

## Abstract

Heterogeneous acquisition changes the information available about a time series, but a fixed token interface does not determine how that information should be represented for conditional generation. We study this question for HSE-conditioned latent Laplace diffusion, separating the physical coefficient posterior used for analysis from the reference latent predicted by the diffusion model. For known linear-Gaussian acquisition, we compare likelihood-natural and posterior-moment headers under the same declared transmission budget. The forward-KL product projection shows that marginal moment blocks are no worse than precision truncation within a fixed partition, while a Schur-complement analysis explains the latter's underestimation of marginal uncertainty. We tighten a joint natural-parameter perturbation bound and derive a computable Gaussian denoiser discrepancy after a fixed target projection. Finite controls demonstrate that coefficient-space rankings need not survive target projection and that phase covariance alone does not justify expensive full blocks. These results motivate target-aware posterior conditioning evaluated against moment, prior-aware low-rank and goal-oriented baselines, with encoder computation and side information reported separately. The present evidence is analytical and sampled-window diagnostic; learned HSE–LLapDiff improvements and real PHM generalization remain to be established.

## 1. Introduction

Industrial monitoring systems observe physical processes through sensors, acquisition filters and sampling schedules. The representation of a signal should therefore reflect both the underlying process and the conditions under which it was measured. Physics-informed learning provides a general route for combining data with scientific structure [@karniadakis2021physics], while representation learning emphasizes retaining factors useful for subsequent tasks rather than preserving every input detail [@bengio2013representation]. For fault diagnosis, HSE introduces a shared embedding interface for heterogeneous signal types, lengths and sampling rates [@li2025hse]. The question addressed here is narrower: what posterior-relevant information should occupy a limited HSE condition when the downstream model generates a specific latent target?

Existing time-series methods handle acquisition heterogeneity in different ways. PatchTST reduces long sequences to local patches [@nie2023patchtst], Neural CDE represents partially observed irregular sequences through continuous-time dynamics [@kidger2020cde], and t-PatchGNN groups varying numbers of observations into temporally aligned patches [@zhang2024tpatchgnn]. Time-IMM further distinguishes causes of irregularity and asynchronous observation [@chang2025timeimm]. These developments make variable observations computationally manageable. They do not, by themselves, determine whether a fixed message preserves the uncertainty needed by a particular conditional target. Moreover, keeping the number of samples per patch fixed is a different intervention from holding its physical duration fixed.

Probabilistic generation supplies a complementary component. Neural Laplace learns dynamics in the Laplace domain [@holt2022neurallaplace], and CSDI models missing values using observed-data-conditioned diffusion [@tashiro2021csdi]. Latent diffusion reduces generation cost by operating on learned autoencoder representations [@rombach2022latent]. LLapDiff applies latent-trajectory diffusion to irregular multivariate time series, with stable modal prediction and gap-aware history conditioning [@you2026llapdiff]. We retain that modeling objective rather than treating its learned latent modes as already identified physical coefficients. Better inference of a physical coefficient vector need not improve the reference latent consumed by the same denoiser.

The distinction between information and computation is central. Approximate sufficient-representation theory already connects conditional KL to downstream conditional diffusion [@oko2025sufficiency]. Score compression also preserves Fisher information under its stated assumptions [@alsing2018compression], although local Fisher preservation is not complete posterior sufficiency. In a known linear-Gaussian acquisition, the information vector and matrix determine the posterior. If the decoder's actual side input already reconstructs the matrix, an explicit coupling token may improve finite-network use of available information, but it does not recover information that was absent. Diffusion training also depends on the stated noise parameterization and loss weighting; an arbitrary denoising score should not be equated with likelihood accuracy [@song2021maximum].

A second distinction is between compressing a likelihood and compressing a posterior for a goal. Prior-aware low-rank approximation already exploits data-informed directions [@spantini2015lowrank], and goal-oriented Bayesian approximation explicitly accounts for a quantity of interest [@spantini2017goal]. This makes a stronger comparison necessary than retaining more entries of an information matrix. At a fixed partition and prior, the product of exact posterior marginals minimizes forward KL over product approximations. It has the same block storage layout as a natural-parameter header, but different encoder computation and update semantics. Omitting this baseline would confound a convenient parameterization with a statistically preferable one.

We therefore evaluate a condition by three declared objects: its actual information access, its target variable, and its total cost. The target is frozen before comparison; all methods receive the same current-acquisition values and side information. Likelihood blocks, posterior moment blocks and low-rank controls are compared first in a known-pole oracle. A Gaussian projection calculation then measures the discrepancy between denoising predictions for the chosen target, rather than assuming that whole-coefficient KL is an adequate surrogate. Coordinate covariance and finite-window support are checked as consistency conditions, not treated as evidence of universal optimality.

The resulting research design uses a target-calibrated moment condition as the fixed-prior analytical reference and tests whether an amortized HSE can exploit related features within its original token budget. The analysis establishes objective-specific controls, a tighter perturbation bound and a target-space denoiser calculation; it does not claim new general sufficiency or Gaussian projection principles. The implemented experiments presently test parameterizations and counterexamples. A learned contribution requires an improvement over the original HSE, a metadata-matched conditioner and the strongest simple posterior head using the same LLapDiff target, denoiser and training budget. Flow Matching is not part of the active method.
