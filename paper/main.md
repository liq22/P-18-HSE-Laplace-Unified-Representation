# Statistically Anchored Conditioning for Latent Laplace Diffusion

## Abstract

A finite time-series condition can have a shared tensor interface without exposing its statistical content in a form that a conditional generator can readily use. We investigate a statistically supervised conditioning intervention for HSE-based Latent Laplace Diffusion. A global readout estimates the first two moments of a declared target functional from a source-supervised ordinary code. A Gaussian conditional score supplies an identification objective, but finite optimization and a positive covariance parameterization do not guarantee calibration. We therefore freeze the selected shared code and moment readout, retain ordinary features alongside the moment fields, and compare against the complete ordinary code trained through the same auxiliary objective. In this deliberately nested comparison, the candidate cannot add Bayes information; its possible benefit is improved use by a finite denoiser. Analytical controls explain why exact moments can lose non-Gaussian shape and why parameter-space posterior rankings need not transfer to the generation target. Native component checks assess gradients, masks, freezing and exact loss conventions. Empirical value remains contingent on a small matched experiment using genuine HSE and frozen-reference exports; no learned HSE-LLapDiff or real-PHM advantage is established by the current interface evidence.

## 1. Introduction

Industrial monitoring systems observe a physical process through sensors, acquisition filters and sampling schedules. The resulting representation must accommodate changes in both the measured signal and the evidence available about it. Physics-informed machine learning offers a general approach to incorporating scientific structure into data-driven models [@karniadakis2021physics], while representation learning asks which factors should remain accessible to downstream tasks [@bengio2013representation]. HSE provides a shared embedding interface for heterogeneous fault-diagnosis signals [@li2025hse]. Our question is narrower than universal representation learning: under a fixed conditioning budget, does an explicit, statistically supervised readout make target-relevant information easier for a conditional generator to use?

Existing time-series models address important computational aspects of heterogeneous observations. PatchTST forms local patches from long sequences [@nie2023patchtst]; Neural CDE represents irregular observations through continuous-time dynamics [@kidger2020cde]; t-PatchGNN accommodates varying observations within temporally aligned patches [@zhang2024tpatchgnn]; and ContiFormer combines continuous-time modeling with attention [@chen2023contiformer]. Time-IMM distinguishes causes of irregular and asynchronous measurement [@chang2025timeimm]. These methods motivate explicit timestamps and masks, but computational compatibility does not by itself establish posterior sufficiency. A valid comparison must also distinguish a fixed number of observed samples from a fixed physical observation duration.

Probabilistic generation provides a complementary capability. Neural Laplace learns representations of dynamics in the Laplace domain [@holt2022neurallaplace], and CSDI conditions diffusion on observed values for probabilistic imputation [@tashiro2021csdi]. Latent diffusion reduces the dimensionality of the generative problem through a learned autoencoder [@rombach2022latent]. LLapDiff combines a latent-trajectory diffusion objective with stable modal prediction and gap-aware history conditioning for irregular multivariate time series [@you2026llapdiff]. We retain its target and generative mechanism. Its reference latent, its denoiser-predicted poles and the physical coefficient vector of a known-pole analytical oracle are different objects; improving an oracle coefficient posterior need not improve the actual reference latent.

Statistical compression already provides strong analytical precedents. Score compression can preserve Fisher information under stated assumptions [@alsing2018compression], and approximate sufficient-representation theory links conditional information loss with conditional generation [@oko2025sufficiency]. Prior-aware and goal-oriented Bayesian approximation further show why one should account for both the prior and the actual quantity of interest [@spantini2015lowrank; @spantini2017goal]. These results preclude claiming that retaining more precision-matrix entries is intrinsically novel or optimal. If the actual decoder side input already reconstructs the acquisition operator, a new explicit field may improve finite computation without recovering previously absent information. Both effects must be separated from the cost of carrying out posterior inference at the encoder.

A further difficulty concerns the meaning of intermediate features. A free denoiser can compensate transformations of its input, so a coordinate named mean or covariance does not acquire that statistical meaning from denoising loss alone. Explicitly supervised intermediate concepts provide a methodological precedent, although concept supervision is not a posterior theorem [@koh2020concept]. Gaussian conditional scoring identifies first and second moments at the unrestricted population optimum, in the framework of statistical scoring [@gneiting2007proper], but exact moments may still hide non-Gaussian shape. Heteroscedastic likelihood optimization can also behave poorly in finite neural models [@seitzer2022pitfalls]. We therefore distinguish the training objective, the statistical interpretation of a frozen readout, and the actual information delivered to the generator.

We study one constrained intervention and a deliberately strong control. A source-supervised shared trunk produces an ordinary code, and a global moment head reads that same code. The auxiliary score therefore trains parameters on the code later consumed by the ordinary-condition baseline, rather than an independent side branch. After source-validation selection, both arms use the same checkpoint and freeze the complete conditioning path. The candidate replaces a declared subset of ordinary coordinates with explicit moment fields while retaining the remaining coordinates; the comparator transmits the complete supervised ordinary code. In this construction the candidate is a deterministic function of the comparator message. Any finite-denoiser advantage must consequently be explained by accessibility or optimization, not additional Bayes information. This restriction belongs to the chosen control and is not a universal ordering of equal-budget encoders.

The contribution under investigation is the statistically anchored conditioner evaluated through this matched native comparison. We use the existing posterior and projection analyses to delimit its interpretation, not to reclaim generic information identities as inventions. Noise schedules, regression parameterizations and loss weights remain part of the actual objective [@nichol2021improved; @song2021maximum]. Native checks assess shared gradients, freezing, field consumption and one-batch loss reconstruction against the original LLapDiff component. Such execution establishes compatibility, not a learned-method advantage. A substantive claim requires a small paired experiment on genuine frozen HSE and reference-encoder exports, with source and unseen acquisitions evaluated separately. Larger SOTA execution and automatic contribution promotion are not justified by a component test alone.
