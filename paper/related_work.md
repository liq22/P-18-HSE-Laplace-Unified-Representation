# 2. Related Work

## 2.1 Heterogeneous representation and cross-dataset diagnosis

HSE addresses heterogeneous industrial inputs, while FISHER uses physical-duration time–frequency windows and sub-band representations [@li2025hse; @fan2026fisher]. TF-ProFM provides a further prototype/time–frequency industrial reference [@pi2026tfprofm]. These works establish strong structured-input baselines. In FISHER's technical report, frozen representations are evaluated with nearest-neighbor classification using labelled training references from the evaluated dataset. This is distinct from diagnosis on a target dataset whose labels are unavailable until final evaluation.

TS-TCC learns through temporal prediction and contextual contrast, whereas TF-C aligns time- and frequency-domain representations [@eldele2021tstcc; @zhang2022tfc]. TF-C includes target fine-tuning in its transfer protocol. Domain Separation Networks learn shared/private features using reconstruction and difference losses, with target-domain access during adaptation [@bousmalis2016dsn]. In contrast, the roles used here describe acquisition-observable subspaces in a declared reference, not statistical domain specificity alone. Invariant-representation analysis and DomainBed further show why label mechanisms and model selection must be held explicit [@zhao2019invariant; @gulrajani2021domainbed]. Our comparison uses source-only fitting and separates acquisition changes from mechanical and class-distribution changes.

## 2.2 Partial-view learning and conditional identification

MVAE combines available modality experts and trains over modality subsets. MMVAE instead uses a mixture-based variational posterior for joint and cross-modal generation [@wu2018mvae; @shi2019mmvae]. Their training design and assumptions supply the cross-view relationships used for prediction. This differs from having separate datasets that each observe only part of a physical state. Even when those datasets share an observed component, the two observed joint marginals need not identify the joint conditional required by a downstream generator.

This distinction is especially important when retaining private evidence. Identifying p(M given C) does not identify p(M given C,P): adding P changes the target conditional rather than merely increasing the input width. Conditional independence can supply the missing relationship, but it is an additional modeling assumption, not a consequence of shared-view alignment. The finite construction in Fig. 1 makes that ambiguity explicit. For learned latent targets, iVAE identifies components under auxiliary-variable and structural conditions, while the unsupervised-disentanglement counterexample demonstrates the ambiguity of unrestricted latent interpretations [@khemakhem2020ivae; @locatello2019disentanglement]. A physical recovery claim therefore needs a calibrated dictionary or validated coordinate correspondence; otherwise the target remains a reference-feature distribution.

## 2.3 Measurement-conditioned diffusion and learning from corrupted sources

DDPM provides Gaussian forward corruption and learned reverse denoising [@ho2020ddpm]. DDRM uses singular directions of a linear noisy measurement model, and DDNM separates range consistency from null-space generation [@kawar2022ddrm; @wang2023ddnm]. DPS approximates likelihood guidance through a denoised estimate and supports noisy nonlinear measurements [@chung2023dps]. Their existing capabilities establish both the usefulness of measurement structure and the distinction between a sampling approximation and an exact posterior.

Ambient Diffusion learns a conditional denoiser using additional corruption under an observation-ensemble condition; the theorem and its practical sampling approximation have different scopes. Consistent Diffusion Meets Tweedie adds consistency to noisy-only distribution learning [@daras2023ambient; @daras2024consistent]. A-DPS then uses an ambient-trained prior for inverse reconstruction with a different acquisition operator [@aali2025ambientdps]. These results make paired clean data one possible identification route rather than a universal prerequisite.

Two closer EM-based methods sharpen the comparison. Rozet et al. learn a diffusion prior by alternating posterior sampling and denoiser fitting; their moment-matching sampler uses Tweedie's covariance with iterative linear algebra [@rozet2024diem]. DiffEM directly learns a conditional diffusion posterior within an EM construction under a known corruption likelihood [@hosseintabar2025diffem]. Their evidence includes corrupted-image generation/reconstruction; Rozet et al. additionally study a known low-dimensional manifold and accelerated MRI. These works already address posterior-mediated learning from incomplete observations. The unresolved question for the present study is narrower: how a source-identified target *under the actual condition*, support restriction and temporal structure jointly affect industrial diagnostic transfer. Convergence of an observed-data objective alone cannot distinguish two source-compatible joint laws such as those in Fig. 1.

## 2.4 Continuous-time structure and missing-value generation

Neural CDE models irregular data through a continuously controlled latent path; Neural Laplace predicts trajectories through a learned Laplace representation and inverse-transform evaluation [@kidger2020cde; @holt2022neurallaplace]. TimeGrad combines recurrent history conditioning with autoregressive diffusion. CSDI conditions a score model on observed entries, and SSSD investigates state-space denoisers with both all-entry and missing-only diffusion [@rasul2021timegrad; @tashiro2021csdi; @alcaraz2023sssd]. Thus, irregular-time modeling and missing-only generation are established capabilities.

LLapDiff supplies the closest temporal generator: its denoiser predicts stable modal parameters and evaluates a latent trajectory at irregular query times [@you2026llapdiff]. This concerns Laplace-domain dynamics, not a Laplace-noise distribution. Avoiding numerical integration over physical time does not eliminate iterative reverse diffusion. Its forecasting and historical-query protocols also fix an observation horizon; a comparator with access to additional future observations solves a different conditional problem. We therefore match query targets, conditioning horizons, noise schedules and output parameterizations when comparing ordinary and Laplace temporal denoisers. Source-reference blocks, not the generator's unconstrained predicted poles, define the proposed physical support restriction.

## 2.5 Posterior compression, uncertainty and diagnostic value

Score compression retains Fisher information in a stated local model. Prior- and goal-oriented low-rank methods optimize Gaussian posterior approximations using the measurement, prior and target geometry [@alsing2018compression; @spantini2015lowrank; @spantini2017goal]. These are analytical references for target-aware inference, rather than evidence that every learned statistical token is sufficient. Approximate-sufficiency theory links conditional information loss to denoising error; predictive V-information makes usefulness depend on the available predictor family [@oko2025sufficiency; @xu2020usable]. The corresponding KL and conditional-expectation identities support our controls without making a new-information claim for a deterministic representation.

Proper scoring rules evaluate a predictive distribution, whereas neural optimization and distribution shift affect whether a learned model attains the desired statistical behavior [@gneiting2007proper; @seitzer2022pitfalls; @ovadia2019uncertainty]. We accordingly separate joint posterior score, uncertainty coverage, diagnostic macro-F1 and computation. Conditional moments are a statistical anchor, not a complete non-Gaussian posterior. Same-head affine and generic nonlinear controls test that anchor; Gaussian and mixture posteriors test whether iterative generation is necessary. A crossed restriction–dynamics design then isolates effects that cannot be identified by comparing only the complete model with an unrestricted baseline.
