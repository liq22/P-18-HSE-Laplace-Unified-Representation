# Theorem 6 — Sufficiency of a posterior-valued representation

## Status

**Proved under Axioms A7 and A9.**

## Purpose

An unobserved physical component cannot generally be encoded honestly by a single point. This theorem shows that the exact posterior distribution of the latent physical state is a sufficient representation for any downstream task that depends on the observation only through that state.

## 1. Setup

Let:

- \(\Theta\) be the latent physical state;
- \(O\) be the heterogeneous observation;
- \(Y\) be a downstream target.

Assume

\[
Y\perp\!\!\!\perp O\mid\Theta.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Define the representation

\[
R(O)=\mathbb P(\Theta\in\cdot\mid O),
\]

which is a random probability measure.

## 2. Lemma 6.1 — task posterior is an integral over the latent posterior

### Statement

For every measurable target event \(B\),

\[
\mathbb P(Y\in B\mid O)
=
\int
\mathbb P(Y\in B\mid\Theta=\theta)
R(O)(d\theta)
\quad\text{almost surely}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

### Proof

Apply the tower property:

\[
\mathbb P(Y\in B\mid O)
=
\mathbb E
\left[
\mathbf 1_{\{Y\in B\}}
\mid O
\right]

=
\mathbb E
\left[
\mathbb E
\left[
\mathbf 1_{\{Y\in B\}}
\mid\Theta,O
\right]
\mid O
\right].
\]

By the conditional independence assumption (6.1),

\[
\mathbb E
\left[
\mathbf 1_{\{Y\in B\}}
\mid\Theta,O
\right]
=
\mathbb P(Y\in B\mid\Theta).
\]

Taking the outer conditional expectation integrates this function with respect to the conditional distribution of \(\Theta\) given \(O\), which is \(R(O)\). This gives Equation (6.2). ∎

## 3. Theorem 6 — posterior representation is sufficient

### Statement

\[
\boxed{
Y\perp\!\!\!\perp O\mid R(O)
}
\]

Equivalently, the conditional distribution of \(Y\) given \(O\) is a measurable function of \(R(O)\) alone.

### Detailed proof

Define, for each measurable target event \(B\), the functional

\[
F_B(\mu)
=
\int
\mathbb P(Y\in B\mid\Theta=\theta)
\mu(d\theta).
\]

By Lemma 6.1,

\[
\mathbb P(Y\in B\mid O)
=
F_B(R(O)).
\tag{6.3}
\]

The right-hand side is measurable with respect to the sigma-algebra generated by \(R(O)\). Therefore

\[
\mathbb P(Y\in B\mid O,R(O))
=
\mathbb P(Y\in B\mid O)
=
F_B(R(O)).
\]

Taking conditional expectation with respect to \(R(O)\),

\[
\mathbb P(Y\in B\mid R(O))
=
F_B(R(O)).
\]

Hence

\[
\mathbb P(Y\in B\mid O,R(O))
=
\mathbb P(Y\in B\mid R(O)),
\]

which is the definition of

\[
Y\perp\!\!\!\perp O\mid R(O).
\]

∎

## 4. Corollary 6.1 — no Bayes decision is lost by the exact posterior

Let \(a\in\mathcal A\) be an action and \(L(a,Y)\) a measurable loss. The Bayes action given \(O\) is

\[
a^*(O)
\in
\arg\min_a
\mathbb E[L(a,Y)\mid O].
\]

By Theorem 6, the conditional law of \(Y\) given \(O\) depends only on \(R(O)\). Thus an equivalent Bayes action can be written as

\[
a^*(O)=\widetilde a^*(R(O)).
\]

The exact posterior-valued representation is therefore sufficient for every downstream decision satisfying the Markov assumption.

## 5. Lemma 6.2 — the posterior mean is not sufficient in general

### Counterexample

Let the latent state be scalar. Consider two observations \(O=a\) and \(O=b\) with posterior distributions

\[
\Theta\mid O=a
\sim
\frac12\delta_{-1}+rac12\delta_{1},
\]

\[
\Theta\mid O=b
=
\delta_0.
\]

Both posterior means equal zero:

\[
\mathbb E[\Theta\mid O=a]
=
\mathbb E[\Theta\mid O=b]
=0.
\]

Let the task be

\[
Y=\mathbf 1\{|\Theta|>1/2\}.
\]

Then

\[
\mathbb P(Y=1\mid O=a)=1,
\qquad
\mathbb P(Y=1\mid O=b)=0.
\]

The posterior mean cannot distinguish the two observations, while the posterior distribution can. Thus replacing an unobserved private block by its mean can destroy task-relevant information. ∎

## 6. Relation to the unified representation

The full ideal representation contains:

\[
R_d(O_d)
=
\operatorname{Law}
\left(
T_d\Theta_c,
\Theta_{p,d},
\Theta_{u,d}
\mid O_d
\right).
\]

The deterministic shared and private blocks can appear as degenerate factors of this measure. The unobserved block remains non-degenerate when the observation does not identify it.

## 7. Failure boundaries

1. If \(Y\) depends directly on sensor identity or missingness beyond \(\Theta\), Equation (6.1) fails.
2. An approximate diffusion posterior is only approximately sufficient.
3. Finite posterior samples may omit rare modes.
4. Posterior sufficiency does not imply that the latent model is physically identifiable.
5. A chosen downstream model may be unable to consume a distribution-valued representation even when the representation is theoretically sufficient.

## 8. Experimental implication

Compare at least:

- posterior mean only;
- mean plus variance;
- Monte Carlo posterior samples;
- an oracle posterior in the known-pole synthetic system.

Use a task whose decision changes with posterior variance or multimodality. If the mean-only representation performs identically on every relevant task, the added diffusion posterior has no demonstrated value for that setting.
