# Task- and Budget-Conditioned Representation Selection for Heterogeneous Time Series

## Abstract

A common time-series embedding shape does not establish that its coordinates are preferable for a particular prediction task. We study conditional-moment reparameterization under explicit consumer, supervision and computation constraints. A complete ordinary code and its derived statistical message share a source-trained checkpoint, so any gain must be distinguished from extra observations, auxiliary supervision and generic coordinate conditioning. Analysis of the implemented affine mean head shows why mean-only messages cannot enlarge an affine consumer family. A coordinate-transported ridge objective supplies an exact reference for full-dimensional PCA, orthogonal and whitening alternatives. An official variable-length speech-feature experiment reproduces the same classification predictions under these matched objectives; whitening with an unchanged penalty changes scores but not the test decisions in this configuration. These results define the simple alternatives that a learned conditional-moment message must exceed. The broader evaluation tests target, consumer and budget dependence across separate clinical, wearable, climate, energy and speech tasks, with industrial evidence retained in the companion TII study.

## 1. Introduction

A useful representation is defined relative to the prediction task and the model that consumes it, not solely by a shared tensor interface. Representation learning has long emphasized this dependence [@bengio2013representation]. Broad time-series models such as MOMENT and UniTS make multi-task and multi-dataset comparisons feasible [@goswami2024moment; @gao2024units]. The remaining question for a proposed structured message is specific: which predictive capability does it provide beyond a comparably trained ordinary code and simple same-dimensional reparameterizations?

Heterogeneous sampling, missingness and channel structure already have strong methodological precedents. Neural CDE, t-PatchGNN, Hi-Patch and HyperIMTS retain temporal structure through different continuous, patch and graph constructions [@kidger2020cde; @zhang2024tpatchgnn; @luo2025hipatch; @li2025hyperimts]. Moirai-MoE uses sparse expert specialization, while AME-TS supervises expert organization with structural descriptors [@liu2025moiraimoe; @wang2026amets]. Thus neither heterogeneous tokenization nor a structurally guided router is, by itself, an unoccupied problem. A new representation must be tested with matched observation access, target definitions and useful simple baselines.

Probabilistic prediction sharpens this requirement. Neural Laplace, CSDI and LLapDiff provide continuous-time or conditional generation mechanisms [@holt2022neurallaplace; @tashiro2021csdi; @you2026llapdiff]. LLapDiff's reference latent, physical modal coefficients in an analytical oracle and a dataset's actual prediction labels are different targets. We retain LLapDiff as one finite consumer rather than assuming that diffusion is necessary for every task. Classification therefore starts with direct label-prediction heads; forecasting and imputation retain simple probability heads and task-specific proper scores. Lower native denoising error cannot substitute for improved classification metrics.

The general theoretical foundations also already exist. Statistical compression and goal-oriented Bayesian approximation address retained evidence and target quantities [@alsing2018compression; @spantini2015lowrank; @spantini2017goal]. Approximate sufficiency relates representations to conditional generation, while predictive V-information explicitly restricts the observer's computation [@oko2025sufficiency; @xu2020usable]. Our controlled construction uses a complete source-supervised code R and a deterministic message M=T(R). Its generic information–fitting decomposition is useful for interpreting a result, but does not favor conditional moments over whitening, a rotation or a small neural transformation.

We consequently examine the actual message structure before adding a larger model. The implemented mean readout is affine in R; a mean-only message followed by an affine consumer cannot represent functions outside the original affine family. Nonlinear covariance fields require their own comparison with generic nonlinear features. A further distinction arises even for invertible affine coordinates: whitening followed by an unchanged isotropic penalty changes the regularized learning problem. Transporting the penalty with the coordinate map yields an exact prediction-equivalence control. This separates a meaningful statistical-message effect from scale, conditioning and regularization effects without assuming that more convenient coordinates contain more information.

The empirical sequence follows that distinction. Source-trained transformations and hyperparameter choices are frozen before test evaluation. Ordinary code, full-dimensional PCA, whitening and seeded orthogonal coordinates precede the learned M/B1-aux comparison; a same-budget linear or small-MLP transformation and field shuffles test statistical specificity. Gaussian scoring identifies conditional moments only under its assumptions, and its finite neural optimization is diagnosed explicitly [@gneiting2007proper; @seitzer2022pitfalls]. Best single and strong static predictive fusion are required before optional acquisition routing. Existing multi-expert and calibration theory informs that secondary comparison but is not claimed as a new general contribution [@mao2024regression; @mao2025routing; @angelopoulos2025ltt].

The first completed external reference uses the official Japanese Vowels sequences and an explicitly simple time-mean LPC representation. Source-fitted full-dimensional coordinate controls and restored ridge predictors give identical test labels in the recorded configuration. This result does not establish an HSE or conditional-moment advantage; it establishes a real data path and a strong coordinate-equivalence check. The remaining domains and genuine learned representations still require their own source-fitted models and task-compatible comparisons. General conclusions must exceed the industrial companion's intervention plus additional datasets, without counting shared results twice.

## 2. Contribution under evaluation

The candidate is a method-specific account of when conditional-moment coordinates help a finite time-series predictor beyond ordinary supervised and generic transformed representations. The affine-consumer and transported-penalty statements are classical supporting analysis, not sufficient TPAMI novelty. A distinct general contribution requires actual target/consumer/budget-dependent findings across more than one encoder and consumer family. No learned advantage or SOTA result is asserted before those experiments.
